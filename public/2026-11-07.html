Text-guided image editing with diffusion models struggles with spatial edits (e.g. manipulating objects, changing viewpoints, manipulating occlusions) that depend on the 3D structure understanding of a scene. Marigold and follow-up work demonstrate that a pretrained diffusion backbone already harbors strong geometric priors that can be activated for depth estimation, yet how to redirect these priors toward editing remains unresolved. Through per-layer gradient probing, we discover that the editing and depth objectives are compatible in early transformer layers but actively conflict in late layers, explaining why naive depth-conditioned editing either ignores geometry or degrades visual quality. We present StitchEdit, which first activates the backbone's geometric capability via a lightweight depth-denoising adapter, then selectively fine-tunes this adapter for editing using a probe-guided per-layer learning rate schedule that preserves depth knowledge where it helps and prunes it where it conflicts. On the proposed StitchEditBench, a benchmark of 150 geometry-dependent edits spanning spatial transformation, occlusion manipulation, and viewpoint change, StitchEdit consistently surpasses both instruction-based and depth-conditioned baselines on perceptual quality and geometric fidelity.

Post-training aims to enhance desired attributes in visual generative models while preserving their general generation capabilities. Reward-guided post-training faces two challenges: real training data may mismatch the generator's output distribution, and attribute supervision may be confounded by unrelated content. Unbounded reward maximization can also cause overoptimization and degrade generation quality. We propose a framework combining Generator-Aligned Reward Modeling (GARM) and Target-Aware Bounded Optimization (TABO). GARM mitigates distribution mismatch through generator-support matching and reduces content confounding using content-matched real contrasts, yielding generator-relevant, attribute-focused reward supervision. TABO maps the learned reward to a smooth, upper-bounded function calibrated from real target-positive examples. This transformation is independent of the optimization algorithm and supports both direct reward-gradient and policy-based optimization. We further introduce Target-Aware GRPO (TA-GRPO), an adaptation of Flow-GRPO that combines fixed stage-wise advantage scaling, a generator-to-target curriculum, and timestep-balanced credit assignment for flow-based video optimization. Experiments show that GARM improves target-attribute expression while maintaining competitive generation quality. TABO better balances attribute enhancement and visual quality than unconstrained reward maximization, while TA-GRPO improves target enhancement over vanilla Flow-GRPO and reduces unintended attribute leakage on neutral prompts. These results highlight the importance of generator-aligned reward supervision and controlled, data-calibrated optimization for targeted visual generation.

Synthetic data from diffusion models can improve object detection, but long-tail performance is still limited by scarce rare classes and inflexible conditioning (e.g., text-only or layout-only inputs). Expanding the Quality-Coverage Frontier with Competitive Flow Matching

Flow matching (FM) generators often face a quality-coverage tradeoff. Inference-time techniques such as classifier-free guidance raise quality, but at the cost of coverage. We hypothesize this limitation stems from a basic property of the objective: an intermediate noisy image could correspond to many clean images, yet the model can only predict a single velocity, at best averaging over the multiple possibilities. To mitigate this, we introduce competition among predictions during training. We achieve this by augmenting the predictor with a randomly drawn low-dimensional conditioning code $z$. For each training example, we sample $K$ values of $z$ to produce $K$ candidate velocities that compete to explain the target, and apply the loss only to the best-matching candidate. This winner-take-all objective allows different modes to shape different predictions, rather than being averaged into a single compromise. Furthermore, this term naturally enables a new form of guidance, auxiliary conditioning scaling, which complements traditional guidance methods. Together, these two guidance mechanisms show that our method substantially improves the quality-coverage frontier. We validate on ImageNet-256, using two different testbeds, SiT and RAEv2. Given less than 3% of the original SiT pretraining compute for post-training, at the 67% Recall point, our method reduces $\mathrm{FD}_{r,6}$ from 19.22 to 5.91. On RAEv2, at the 70% Recall point, it reduces $\mathrm{FD}_{r,6}$ from 16.83 to 3.18.

Same Layout, Different Count: What Number Words Do Past Ten in Text-to-Image Models

相同布局，不同数量：文本到图像模型中超过十的数字词起什么作用

Liang Yue, Eric A. Vance

核心贡献: 论文系统研究了五个开源文本到图像模型在物体数量超过约十个后对数字词的处理能力，揭示了模型存在“跟随上限”（reach），并指出超过该上限后起始噪声的粗粒度部分决定图像布局而非物体数量，数字词虽仍影响去噪过程却无法给出明确方向。
方法: 在五个开源文本到图像模型中，固定物体和起始噪声，逐一改变单个变量进行受控实验，并由未见过原始请求的标注者对3,088张图像进行计数。通过比较不同噪声粒度重绘、将数量以带噪网格形式嵌入起始潜变量等方式，分析数字词对生成结果的影响，并对比精确匹配分数与相对误差分数等评价指标。
关键发现: 所有模型在小数量时能跟随数字词，但存在跟随上限：SD3.5-M和FLUX.1-dev在十之后不久便停止跟随，FLUX.2 [klein]和Qwen-Image可松散跟随至二十五。超过上限后，起始噪声的粗粒度部分固定了图像构图但不决定物体数量；仅重绘比八分之一图像更细的噪声即可保持布局，且已使计数偏移达三分之二。数字词仍能到达去噪器并在最初几步起作用，但无法产生明确方向。以带噪网格形式呈现数量时，59%的图像可被识别出数量，而仅用文字告知时仅4%。超过十后，精确匹配分数难以区分松散跟随与忽略数字词的模型，相对误差分数甚至可能偏好恒定猜测十六，需采用同噪声少样本测试加以区分。

Ask SD3.5-Medium for twelve of an object and then, from the same starting noise, for twenty-five. The picture keeps its layout, but the count changes about as much as it would under a new random seed, and in no consistent direction. We study what number words do once a request passes about ten objects, in five open text-to-image models. Holding the object and the starting noise fixed, we change one thing at a time, and people who never see the request count 3,088 of the images. Every model follows the number for small crowds, but only up to a point, its reach: SD3.5-M and FLUX.1-dev stop following it soon after ten, while FLUX.2 [klein] and Qwen-Image follow it loosely up to twenty-five. Beyond SD3.5-M's reach, the coarse part of the starting noise fixes the composition of the picture but not how many objects it holds: redrawing only the noise finer than an eighth of the image keeps the layout as well as a new number does, and already moves the count two-thirds as far. The number word still reaches the denoiser and acts in its first steps, but nothing we tried gives it a clear direction. Shown instead as a noised grid in the starting latent, the count is drawn: people find it in 59% of images, against 4% when it is only told. Past ten, an exact-match score barely separates a model that follows the number loosely from one that ignores it, and a relative-error score can even prefer a constant guess of sixteen. A same-noise test with a few requests per object tells them apart.
