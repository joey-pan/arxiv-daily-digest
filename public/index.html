WeLM-Image: A Unified Image Generation Model towards Visual Intelligence

We present \textbf{WeLM-Image}, a unified image foundation model that integrates multimodal understanding into text-to-image generation, instruction-guided editing, and multi-reference synthesis. Designed for single-GPU deployment in WeChat's large-scale consumer applications, the model processes text, reference images, and target latents as one interleaved sequence. A Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) backbone combines shared attention with independent understanding and generation parameters to limit optimization interference. Equipping each path with Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) modules provides sparse capacity, with 31.7B stored parameters and approximately 5.4B generation parameters activated per target latent token. A generation-oriented data engine combines a knowledge-grounded visual vocabulary with large-scale visual clustering to balance semantic coverage and editing tasks, while training-time evaluation guides iterative data refinement. Bilingual annotations and verified editing supervision support progressive training up to native 1.5K resolution. We optimize generation and editing separately with rubric- and checklist-based rewards, then consolidate the specialized policies through noise-aware on-policy distillation. In the reported overall comparisons, the 1536-resolution model ranks first among the evaluated open-source models on PRISM-Bench, Qwen-Image-Bench, and UnicBench. MeanFlow-based few-step distillation yields WeLM-Image-Turbo, which generates a $1024\times1024$ image in 4.98\,s on a single NVIDIA H20 GPU using eight guidance-free steps.

FisherDistill: Diffusion Distribution Distillation via Closed-Form Fisher Critics

FisherDistill：基于闭式Fisher判别器的扩散分布蒸馏

Mahesh Bhosale, Yuanhao Zhai, Siwei Lyu, Junsong Yuan, David Doermann 等 (6 位作者)

核心贡献: 论文提出FisherDistill，一种无需学习对抗判别器的扩散蒸馏精炼方法，利用闭式Fisher线性判别分析从冻结教师模型的中间表示中提取轻量级精炼信号，以弥补蒸馏生成器与多步教师之间的分布差距。
方法: 该方法基于Fisher线性判别分析，利用冻结教师模型的中间表示估计真实样本与生成样本的一阶和二阶统计量，推导出一个闭式Fisher方向，使两类特征分布相对于类内变异最大化分离。该方向提供轻量级精炼信号，补充原始蒸馏目标，无需额外对抗判别器及其优化循环。方法可跨多种蒸馏框架使用。

Recent advances in diffusion distillation have enabled high-quality image generation in only one or a few network function evaluations (NFEs). Despite their efficiency, aggressively distilled generators often retain a residual distribution gap from their multi-step teachers, motivating recent methods to introduce adversarial refinement with a learned discriminator. In this work, we ask whether such refinement necessarily requires a learned adversarial discriminator. We introduce \textbf{FisherDistill}, a simple alternative based on Fisher linear discriminant analysis. Using intermediate representations from the frozen teacher, we estimate the first- and second-order statistics of real and generated samples and derive a closed-form Fisher direction that maximally separates their feature distributions relative to within-class variation. This direction provides a lightweight refinement signal that complements the original distillation objective without requiring an additional adversarial discriminator or its optimization loop. Across multiple distillation frameworks, our approach consistently improves generation quality over their respective distilled baselines and substantially closes the gap to adversarially refined counterparts. Learning Noise Optimization for Reward Alignment in Few-Step Generative Models

面向少步生成模型奖励对齐的学习噪声优化

Hongeun Kim, Jeongsol Kim, Jong Chul Ye

核心贡献: 提出LeNO方法，将奖励引导的初始噪声优化摊销为学习到的残差传输，在保持预训练生成器冻结的情况下，实现少步生成模型的高效奖励对齐，推理时仅需单次噪声校正而无需迭代搜索。

查看原文摘要

Reward alignment for diffusion- and flow-based generative models is often formulated over stochastic trajectories, making it less suitable for few-step deterministic generators. Initial noise optimization provides a trajectory-free alternative, but existing gradient-free methods require costly per-sample search at inference. We introduce LeNO (Learning Noise Optimization), which amortizes reward-guided initial noise optimization into a learned residual transport while keeping the pretrained generator frozen. LeNO learns an anchor-conditioned distribution over noise corrections using Local GRPO and scalar rewards, eliminating iterative test-time optimization. For Gaussian initial noise with norm-preserving projection, we derive a tractable upper bound on the KL divergence between the corrected noise distribution and the original prior, providing a principled regularization objective. We evaluate LeNO on few-step text-to-image and protein structure generation, including expensive black-box designability rewards. Across both domains, LeNO improves target rewards with a single noise correction at inference while largely preserving generation quality and diversity.

Scientific figures give research ideas a visual form through choices of abstraction, composition, and emphasis. Generative AI offers new ways to realize these choices, raising questions about explanatory clarity and purposeful visual expression. We examine these questions through a visual retrospective of 371 motivation and framework figures from 200 papers. The corpus comprises a quota-based main sample of 160 papers spanning four temporal periods and a separate purposive style-contrast sample of 40 papers. A single multimodal judge evaluates six dimensions of communication and design, with three repeated judgments per figure. Gains in the six-dimension average are larger for main-sample motivation figures than for frameworks. Case analyses show that added explanation can clarify comparisons while leaving typographic hierarchy and visual economy unresolved. These model-based findings support researcher-led design, with AI assisting exploration and authors retaining responsibility for explanatory purpose, composition, and scientific relationships. FewStepRL: Fine-tuning Few-step Diffusion Models with Reinforcement Learning

FewStepRL：用强化学习微调少步扩散模型

Haitao Lin, Minsi Ren, Yang Li, Xingtong Ge, Zheyuan Hu 等 (7 位作者)

核心贡献: 论文系统性地将强化学习目标适配到少步扩散模型，提出“变换—分解—适配”三阶段框架，将多种主流RL目标统一为少步版本，并在一个模块化代码库中实现。
方法: 首先通过变换将基于密度、速度或奖励的目标统一表达为x空间中的共同策略预测；然后分解将各目标的梯度写成向参考的自回归项与奖励增强项之和，使方法在梯度层面归结为锚点与权重的选择；最后适配阶段从少步模型的传输终点或切线上计算锚点，瞬时速度可由网络自身或辅助网络恢复。由此得到FlowGRPO、AWM、DiffusionNFT、DiffusionOPSD和ReFL的少步版本。
关键发现: 实验表明，当奖励可微且梯度计算代价可接受时，基于奖励梯度的方法最有效；否则DiffusionNFT类方法通常能在较少迭代内达到较高奖励，尽管单次迭代成本有时更高。额外增加推理步数在总体上没有系统性收益，但部分输出在采样步数为训练时的2至4倍时可见改善。

Reinforcement learning (RL) has become a standard post-training method for diffusion models, but most RL objectives roll out the current policy at every iteration, and with a many-step sampler these rollouts typically dominate the cost of training. Few-step models cut this cost, yet they model an average velocity over an interval, whereas existing RL objectives are built for an instantaneous velocity with a many-step sampler. We adapt RL objectives to few-step models systematically in three phases, namely a transformation, a decomposition and an adaptation. The transformation to $x$-space expresses each objective we consider through a common policy prediction, at the clean end by default and at an explicitly marked level for transition or flow-map objectives, so that objectives stated over densities, velocities or rewards become comparable. The decomposition writes the gradient of each of these objectives as the sum of a self-regression toward a reference and a reward augmentation, so that a method reduces, at the gradient level, to a choice of anchors and weights. The adaptation computes each anchor from a few-step model at the end of its transport or along its tangent, with the instantaneous velocity recovered from the network itself or from an auxiliary network. This yields few-step versions of FlowGRPO, AWM, DiffusionNFT, DiffusionOPSD and ReFL in one modular codebase. In our experiments, reward-gradient-based methods are the most effective when the reward is differentiable and its gradient is affordable; otherwise DiffusionNFT-style methods generally reach high rewards in relatively few iterations despite a sometimes higher per-iteration cost. Extra inference steps show no systematic aggregate benefit in our experiments, although selected outputs visibly improve with two to four times as many sampling steps as in training.
