Squaring-the-Sphere: Distortion-Free and Seamless Panorama Generation via CubeAtlas

化球为方：基于CubeAtlas的无畸变无缝全景图生成

Xuhui Zuo, Chenjie Cao, Tengfei Wang, Junta Wu, Yisu Zhang 等 (6 位作者)

核心贡献: 提出了一种名为CubeAtlas的全新全景图表示方法，将立方体贴图的六个面展开为单一紧凑画布，在保持透视几何的同时实现无缝全景生成，并引入布局感知的稀疏注意力机制以降低计算成本。
方法: CubeAtlas将立方体贴图的赤道面与经过旋转对齐的极区面排列在同一画布上，保留跨面关系以支持无缝拼接。基于此表示，预训练扩散Transformer可在单次前向中联合建模完整全景图，无需修改骨干架构或位置编码。针对CubeAtlas的结构化布局，设计了布局感知的稀疏注意力掩码，移除几何无关的token对，保留全景连续性所需的交互。此外，引入免训练超分辨率技术和融合方案，在无畸变透视空间中增强细节后再生成最终ERP全景图。
关键发现: 实验表明，该方法在几何保真度、跨视角一致性和高分辨率视觉质量上优于先前方法；CubeAtlas在token数量与ERP相当的情况下，以更低的注意力计算量实现了优于ERP建模的质量。

Generating 360-degree panoramas requires both local perspective fidelity and global spherical continuity. Existing representations face a trade-off: equirectangular projection (ERP) enables seamless panorama modeling but suffers from severe distortion and a domain gap to pre-trained image generators, whereas cubemaps preserve perspective geometry but often produce inconsistent faces and visible seams. We present Squaring-the-Sphere, a panoramic image generation framework built on a novel representation CubeAtlas, which unfolds all cubemap faces into a single compact canvas. By arranging orientation-aligned, rotated polar regions alongside the equatorial faces, this representation preserves the perspective geometry of cubemaps while organizing the cross-face relationships required for seamless panorama assembly. It further enables a pretrained diffusion transformer to model the complete panorama jointly in a single pass, without altering the backbone architecture or positional encoding. While CubeAtlas has a token count comparable to ERP, its structured layout makes many dense attention interactions unnecessary. We therefore introduce a layout-aware sparse attention mask that preserves the interactions required for panorama continuity while removing geometrically irrelevant token pairs. As a result, CubeAtlas achieves better quality than ERP modeling with lower attention computation. Furthermore, we introduce a training-free super-resolution technique and a fusion scheme that enhance fine details in distortion-free perspective space before achieving the final ERP panorama. Experiments on various benchmarks demonstrate that our method improves geometric fidelity, cross-view consistency, and high-resolution visual quality over previous methods.

HimmBO: High-Dimensional Interactive Model-Merging Bayesian Optimization for Image Generation

Jinyuan Mao, Zhihao Shen, Chenxi Liu

核心贡献: 提出一种两阶段方法HimmBO，将偏好驱动的适配器合并扩展到更大规模的适配器集合，同时大幅降低图像生成成本。
方法: 该方法分为筛选阶段和精炼阶段。筛选阶段通过对预生成图像进行水平集估计来恢复活跃适配器集合，无需生成新图像；精炼阶段在恢复的低维子空间中优化合并系数。两个阶段均使用风格感知核，使偏好信息能在视觉相似的适配器之间迁移。

Fine-tuning has become a common way to customize text-to-image models, leading to community collections of over 100K lightweight adapters. Adapters sharing a base model can be merged with continuous coefficients. This turns a large collection into a high-dimensional continuous design space, which recent work explores interactively using preferential Bayesian optimization (PBO). However, existing approaches are limited to a few dozen preselected adapters and require expensive image generation in every interaction round. We introduce HimmBO, a two-stage method that scales preference-driven adapter merging to much larger collections while reducing image-generation cost. Its screening stage recovers the active adapter set through level-set estimation over pre-generated images, requiring no image generation. The refinement stage then optimizes merge coefficients in the recovered low-dimensional subspace. Both stages use a style-aware kernel, allowing preference information to transfer between visually similar adapters. In simulated-user image-matching experiments, HimmBO achieves higher final similarity than all comparisons while reaching a given similarity with under one tenth of the image generations required by prior work. Its performance remains stable as the collection grows to 1080 adapters, while competing methods degrade substantially.

Frontier coding agents are becoming increasingly adept at 3D modeling. Yet, they still face challenges when building objects meant to be assembled in the physical world, often creating invalid assemblies with floating, colliding, or disconnected parts. We argue that providing agents with tools for assembling builds and verifying their physical feasibility is sufficient for them to produce physically valid assemblies that still adhere to input prompts. Real-image editing commonly relies on inversion and iterative sampling, while efficient one-step editors often stabilize a large update by estimating and averaging editing fields at multiple noise levels. We ask a stricter question: is a single temporal field evaluation sufficient for reliable inversion-free editing? Our answer rests on a previously underused duality in a variance-preserving (VP) one-step model: one source-target query at a shared noisy state simultaneously determines a local angular semantic tangent and a clean endpoint displacement. We integrate the tangent over the analytically known remaining VP angle to obtain a nominal one-step transport, and use the endpoint displacement to correct its long-horizon extrapolation through a constrained projection. This transport-first, correction-second construction yields Endpoint-Calibrated Transport (ECT), a closed-form, training-free editor requiring only one noise level, two text conditions packed into one denoiser batch, and one latent update. ECT requires no image inversion, trajectory integration, second temporal observation, or iterative optimization, and can be directly applied to pretrained epsilon-prediction VP one-step generators. Across the reported benchmark settings, ECT delivers stable gains in editing quality within an extremely short editing time.

Training-free few-step samplers are often described as nearly quality-lossless under conventional FID/CLIP-style evaluation. It remains unclear whether that conclusion transfers to more realistic and complex generation-quality benchmarks. We first systematize the quality metrics used to evaluate training-free sampling acceleration for diffusion and flow-matching models. We find that representative training-free step-shortening methods often remain nearly lossless under FID/CLIP-class metrics, yet can suffer substantial quality degradation when evaluated with semantic-composition, dense-instruction, and long-text-rendering benchmarks such as GenEval, DPG-Bench, and LongTextBench. Parallel sampling is a notable exception: by retaining the full-step discretization and removing only the solver's temporal dependency, it can preserve near-lossless quality while accelerating sampling. Building on this observation, we propose \textit{ParaLin++}, a step-parity parallel sampler that preserves the full-step discretization while accelerating the solver iterations. Experiments across Stable Diffusion-v2, LSUN Church, FLUX.1-dev, and Ming-Flash-Omni 2.0 show that ParaLin++ preserves the full-step Euler quality profile at $2.6\times$--$4.8\times$, $1.68\times$ on FLUX, and $1.21\times$ on Ming.
